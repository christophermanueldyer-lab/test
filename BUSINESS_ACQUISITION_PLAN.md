# Business Acquisition Plan: Your Gameplan for 2026

This plan is designed to take you from zero knowledge to being ready to acquire a small business within 12 months. It covers education, financial preparation, search strategy, due diligence, legal protection, and execution.

**Timeline Goal:** Ready to close on a business by end of 2026
**Status:** Beginner (financial resources available, knowledge to be built)

### Understand Financing Options

**1. SBA 7(a) Loans**
- Up to $5 million for business acquisition
- Typically requires 10-20% down payment
- Personal guarantee required
- Best for established, profitable businesses

**2. Seller Financing**
- Seller holds a note for portion of purchase price
- Common: 20-40% of deal value
- Shows seller confidence in business
- Lower interest than traditional loans

**3. Earnouts**
- Pay based on future performance
- Reduces upfront capital needs
- Aligns seller's incentives with smooth transition
- Typical: 10-30% of deal tied to performance

**4. SBA 504 Loans**
- For real estate and equipment
- Lower down payment (10%)
- Fixed rates, long terms

### Build Your Financial Team

**Must-Have Advisors:**
1. **Accountant/CPA** - Familiar with business acquisitions
2. **Lender/Banker** - Preferably SBA-experienced
3. **Financial Advisor** - To protect personal finances during transition

## Phase 3: Define Your Search Criteria (Month 3-4)

### The "Search Fund" Approach

Rather than looking for any business, define specific criteria to narrow your search.

**Industry Criteria:**
- Industries you understand or can learn quickly
- Industries you're passionate about (you'll live this daily)
- Avoid highly technical industries without relevant experience
- Consider: service businesses, manufacturing, distribution, franchises

**Business Size:**
- **Revenue Range:** $500K - $5M annually (good for first acquisition)
- **EBITDA (profit):** $100K - $750K
- **Purchase Price:** Based on your financing capability
- **Employee Count:** 5-50 employees (manageable)

**Geographic Criteria:**
- Within 50 miles of home (initially, hands-on needed)
- OR fully remote/online businesses
- Consider: Are you willing to relocate?

**Business Quality Indicators:**
- Profitable for 3+ consecutive years
- Diversified customer base (no single customer >15% revenue)
- Recurring revenue model preferred
- Owner not deeply embedded in operations
- Documented processes and procedures
- Growth potential or stability

**Red Flags to Avoid:**
- Declining revenue trend
- Single customer dependency
- Owner does all sales/relationships
- Industry in structural decline
- Pending litigation
- Environmental liabilities
- Heavy regulatory compliance (unless you have expertise)

### Your Search Profile Template

```
TARGET BUSINESS PROFILE:
- Industry: [e.g., HVAC services, light manufacturing, SaaS]
- Revenue: $[X] - $[Y]
- EBITDA: $[X] - $[Y]
- Location: [Specific regions]

- Must-haves: [Recurring revenue, documented processes, etc.]
- Deal-breakers: [Declining revenue, etc.]
```

**Business Brokers:**

- Build relationships with 5-10 brokers
- Tell them your exact criteria
- Check in monthly

**Direct Outreach:**
- Identify 100 businesses that fit your profile
- Send letters or emails to owners
- "I'm looking to buy a business like yours..."
- Lower competition than broker deals

**Network:**
- Tell everyone you're looking to buy
- Accountants, lawyers, bankers know business owners
- Industry associations and events
- Chamber of Commerce

### Initial Screening Process

**When you find a potential deal:**

1. **Request Offering Memorandum (OM) / CIM**
   - Business overview and financials
   - Sign NDA to receive

2. **10-Minute Screen**
   - Does revenue/profit match your criteria?
   - Is industry acceptable?
   - Is location workable?
   - Any obvious red flags?

3. **Deep Dive (if passes screen):**
   - Analyze 3 years of financials
   - Calculate: SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings)
   - Understand revenue drivers
   - Research industry and competition
   - Calculate rough valuation

**Valuation Rule of Thumb:**
Most small businesses sell for 2-4x SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings)
- SDE = EBITDA + Owner Salary + Owner Benefits + Non-recurring expenses

## Phase 5: Due Diligence Framework (Ongoing)

### Build Your Due Diligence Checklist

Once you find a business you want to pursue, thorough due diligence is critical.

**Financial Due Diligence:**
- [ ] 3-5 years of tax returns (business and owner)
- [ ] 3-5 years of P&L statements
- [ ] 3-5 years of balance sheets
- [ ] Current A/R and A/P aging reports
- [ ] Revenue by customer (concentration risk)
- [ ] Reconcile bank statements to financials
- [ ] Verify all "add-backs" claimed by seller
- [ ] Understand all debt and liabilities
- [ ] Review all contracts (leases, vendors, customers)

**Operational Due Diligence:**
- [ ] Organization chart and employee list
- [ ] Key employee contracts and compensation
- [ ] Documented processes and procedures (SOPs)
- [ ] Customer retention rates
- [ ] Supplier relationships and contracts
- [ ] Technology systems and licenses
- [ ] Inventory count and valuation
- [ ] Facilities and equipment condition

**Legal Due Diligence:**
- [ ] Corporate structure and good standing
- [ ] Intellectual property ownership
- [ ] Pending or threatened litigation
- [ ] Compliance with regulations
- [ ] Employment agreements and benefits
- [ ] Environmental issues
- [ ] Insurance policies and claims history

**Market Due Diligence:**
- [ ] Industry trends and outlook
- [ ] Competitive landscape
- [ ] Market size and growth
- [ ] Regulatory changes pending
- [ ] Technology disruption risks

**Quality of Earnings (QoE) Report:**
For deals >$1M, consider hiring a QoE firm to verify financials ($10K-$25K investment)

### Red Flags That Kill Deals
- Material misrepresentation of financials
- Undisclosed liabilities
- Customer concentration >30% in top 3 customers
- Owner is the business (can't be transitioned)
- Pending lawsuits
- Declining industry
- Facilities in poor condition requiring major capex

### Action Items - Due Diligence Phase
- [ ] Create your due diligence checklist (customize from above)
- [ ] Identify a QoE firm for potential use
- [ ] Build a model to analyze business financials
- [ ] Learn to read and interpret financial statements

## Phase 7: Making an Offer & Negotiation (Months 6-9)

### Letter of Intent (LOI)

Once you find the right business, you'll submit a non-binding LOI.

**Key LOI Terms:**
- Purchase price and structure
- Down payment amount
- Seller financing terms (if any)
- Earnout structure (if any)
- Due diligence period (60-90 days typical)
- Assets vs. stock purchase
- Non-compete agreement
- Transition assistance period
- Contingencies (financing, due diligence)
- Exclusivity period

**LOI Negotiation Strategy:**
- Start with fair offer based on market multiples
- Build relationship with seller (not adversarial)
- Understand seller's motivations (retirement, burnout, health)
- Be prepared to walk away
- Leave some room to negotiate but don't lowball

### Deal Structure Options

**Asset Purchase vs. Stock Purchase:**
- **Asset Purchase** (preferred for buyer):
  - Buy assets, not entity
  - Avoid unknown liabilities
  - Better tax treatment
  - Most small business deals

- **Stock Purchase** (preferred for seller):
  - Buy entire entity
  - Inherit all liabilities
  - Simpler for licensed businesses
  - Better for seller tax-wise

**Payment Structure:**
- All cash at close (rare, requires full financing)
- Cash + seller financing
- Cash + earnout
- Combination of all three

**Example Deal Structure:**
```
Purchase Price: $1,000,000
- Down Payment (10%): $100,000 (your cash)
- SBA Loan (70%): $700,000
- Seller Financing (15%): $150,000 (5 years, 6% interest)
- Earnout (5%): $50,000 (based on year 1 revenue)
```

### Negotiation Tactics

**Before Negotiating:**
- Know your BATNA (Best Alternative To Negotiated Agreement)
- Understand seller's motivation
- Have financing pre-approved
- Know market comps

**During Negotiation:**
- Build rapport with seller
- Ask questions, understand their perspective
- Be fair but firm
- Everything is negotiable (price, terms, transition, non-compete)
- Get creative with structure
- Don't be emotional

**Key Items to Negotiate:**
1. Purchase price
2. Seller financing terms and rate
3. Transition period (3-6 months is standard)
4. Non-compete duration and scope (3-5 years typical)
5. Working capital required at close
6. Employee retention requirements
7. Earnout metrics and measurement

## Phase 8: Legal Protection & Closing (Months 8-10)

### Purchase Agreement (Asset Purchase Agreement / Stock Purchase Agreement)

Your attorney will draft or review this. Key sections:

**Representations & Warranties:**
- Seller guarantees facts about business
- Financial accuracy
- No undisclosed liabilities
- Compliance with laws
- Your main protection

**Indemnification:**
- Seller agrees to cover certain losses after closing
- Typically 10-20% of purchase price
- Held in escrow for 12-24 months
- Covers breaches of representations

**Non-Compete Agreement:**
- Prevents seller from competing
- Typical: 3-5 years, within 50-100 miles
- Must be reasonable to be enforceable

**Transition Services Agreement:**
- Seller helps with transition
- Typical: 3-6 months, 20-40 hours/week
- Paid or part of deal terms
- Critical for success

**Closing Conditions:**
- Financing approval
- No material adverse changes
- Key employees agree to stay
- Landlord approves lease assignment

### Escrow and Holdbacks

**Indemnity Escrow:**
- Hold 10-20% of purchase price
- For 12-24 months
- Protects against misrepresentations

**Working Capital Adjustment:**
- Ensure business has sufficient working capital
- Usually adjusted post-closing
- Based on balance sheet at close

### Final Due Diligence

In the 60-90 days between LOI and closing:

- Complete all due diligence items
- QoE report completed
- Finalize financing
- Draft and negotiate purchase agreement
- Obtain landlord consent (if leasing)
- Customer/supplier notifications planned
- Key employee retention plans
- Insurance policies transferred/obtained
- Licenses and permits transferred

### Closing Day

**What Happens:**
- Sign final documents (20+ documents typical)
- Funds transferred to escrow
- Escrow releases funds to seller
- Assets/stock transferred to you
- You're now a business owner!

**Documents You'll Sign:**
- Asset Purchase Agreement / Stock Purchase Agreement
- Bill of Sale
- Non-Compete Agreement
- Transition Services Agreement
- Promissory Note (if seller financing)
- SBA loan documents (many)
- Lease assignment
- Employment agreements with key staff

## Phase 9: Post-Acquisition Transition (Months 10-12 and beyond)

### First 100 Days Critical

**Week 1: Observe & Learn**
- Shadow seller daily
- Meet all employees individually
- Don't make changes yet
- Listen more than talk
- Learn the culture

**Weeks 2-4: Stakeholder Meetings**
- Meet top 10 customers (with seller)
- Meet key suppliers
- Review all contracts and commitments
- Understand employee roles and concerns
- Assess immediate risks

**Months 2-3: Stabilize**
- Maintain status quo on operations
- Implement basic financial reporting if lacking
- Ensure cash flow management
- Address any urgent issues only
- Build trust with team

**Month 4+: Optimize**
- Only now start making improvements
- Implement changes incrementally
- Communicate changes clearly
- Quick wins to build confidence
- Longer-term strategic improvements

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Changing too much, too fast** - Biggest mistake new owners make
2. **Ignoring existing culture** - Respect what's worked
3. **Not listening to employees** - They know the business
4. **Letting seller leave too quickly** - Keep them engaged
5. **Failing to maintain customer relationships** - Do this immediately
6. **Inadequate cash reserves** - Keep 6 months operating capital
7. **Over-leveraging** - Don't take on too much debt

### Your New Owner Responsibilities

**Daily/Weekly:**
- Review cash position
- Monitor key metrics (sales, AR/AP)
- Communicate with team
- Handle key customer issues
- Review pipeline/backlog

**Monthly:**
- Financial statement review
- Team meetings
- Customer check-ins
- Supplier negotiations
- Strategic planning

**Quarterly:**
- Comprehensive financial analysis
- Budget vs. actual review
- Strategic initiatives assessment
- Employee reviews
- Business planning updates

## Phase 10: Protecting Yourself

### Legal Protections

**In the Purchase Agreement:**
- Strong representations and warranties
- Indemnification provisions (12-24 months)
- Escrow holdback (10-20% of price)
- Non-compete agreement (3-5 years)
- Seller transition commitment in writing

**Entity Structure:**
- Buy through LLC or corporation (not personally)
- Limits personal liability
- Set up before making offers

**Insurance:**
- General liability
- Professional liability (if applicable)
- Key person insurance
- Directors & Officers insurance
- Property insurance
- Cyber insurance (if handling data)

### Financial Protections

**Due Diligence:**
- Hire QoE firm for deals >$1M
- Verify every financial claim
- Test revenue and customer claims
- Audit inventory
- Reconcile bank statements to financials

**Deal Structure:**
- Earnouts align seller incentives
- Seller financing keeps seller motivated
- Escrow protects against misrepresentation
- SBA loans have strong underwriting

**Personal Financial Safety:**
- Don't invest 100% of net worth
- Keep 6-12 months personal expenses in reserve
- Don't guarantee debt with personal assets if avoidable (though SBA requires personal guarantee)
- Have health insurance and disability insurance

### Operational Protections

**Transition:**
- 3-6 month seller transition (minimum)
- Customer introduction plan
- Documented processes (SOPs)
- Key employee retention agreements
- Supplier relationship introductions

**Contingency Planning:**
- Identify single points of failure
- Cross-train employees
- Build relationships with key stakeholders
- Maintain adequate working capital
- Have backup plans for critical functions

### Red Flags That Should Stop a Deal

**Walk away if you discover:**
- Material financial misrepresentation
- Undisclosed litigation
- Environmental issues
- Seller won't provide transition support
- Business is entirely dependent on seller's relationships
- Major customer planning to leave
- Regulatory violations
- Evidence of fraud
- Your gut says something is wrong

**Trust your instincts.** If something feels wrong, it probably is.

## Conclusion

Buying a small business is one of the best wealth-building strategies available. With financial resources already available, your main task is education and execution.

**Key Success Factors:**
1. **Education** - Invest time upfront learning the process
2. **Patience** - Don't rush into wrong deal; right deal takes time
3. **Team** - Hire experienced advisors; don't go alone
4. **Due Diligence** - Trust but verify everything
5. **Discipline** - Stick to your criteria; be willing to walk away
6. **Transition** - Success happens post-close with good transition

**Remember:**
- This is a marathon, not a sprint
- Most buyers look at 100+ businesses before buying one
- Your first offer probably won't be accepted
- Due diligence will uncover issues - that's normal
- The right deal will feel right (and the numbers will work)

**You can do this.** Thousands of people buy their first business every year. With preparation, discipline, and the right advisors, you'll be ready to close on a business in 2026.

6. What are the biggest challenges in the business?

8. Tell me about the team and key employees
9. What would you do differently if you were staying?
10. What does a new owner need to know to be successful?
