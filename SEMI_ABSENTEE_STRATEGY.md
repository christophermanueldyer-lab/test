## The Semi-Absentee Reality Check

### What Semi-Absentee Actually Means

**Important Truth:** True "passive" businesses are rare. Semi-absentee typically means:
- 5-10 hours/week minimum (not zero)
- Strong existing management team in place
- Owner handles strategy, finance, key relationships
- Manager handles day-to-day operations
- You're the "CEO," not the operator

**Your Time Commitment:**
- First 3-6 months: 20-30 hours/week (transition period)
- Months 6-12: 10-15 hours/week (stabilization)
- Year 2+: 5-10 hours/week (true semi-absentee)

**Critical Success Factor:**
The business MUST have a strong operator/manager already in place who will stay post-acquisition.

## Financing Strategy: Maximize Seller Financing

### Why Sellers Finance

Sellers agree to financing when:
1. **Market conditions** - Hard to find all-cash buyers
2. **Tax benefits** - Spread capital gains over multiple years
3. **Business characteristics** - Hard to get bank financing (your advantage!)
4. **Retirement income** - Want steady income stream
5. **Confidence** - Believe business will continue succeeding

### Your Negotiation Advantage

**You're an attractive buyer because:**
- Have management/subscription experience (de-risks deal)
- Want to keep your job (shows financial stability)
- Not desperate (can wait for right deal)
- Can close quickly (no bank approval needed)
- Reasonable down payment shows commitment

### Seller Financing Deal Structures

**Structure #1: Aggressive (75-80% Seller Financed)**
```
Purchase Price: $300,000
Down: $75,000 (25%)
Seller Note: $225,000 (75%)
Terms: 7-10 years, 6-7% interest
Payment: ~$3,200/month

When this works:
- Motivated seller (retirement, health, burnout)
- Strong cash flow (can service debt)
- Business hard to get bank financing
- You have strong background
```

**Structure #2: Hybrid (50% Seller, 25% Bank, 25% Down)**
```
Purchase Price: $400,000
Down: $100,000 (25%)
Bank Loan: $200,000 (50%) - could be SBA or local bank
Seller Note: $100,000 (25%)
Seller Terms: 5 years, 5% interest

When this works:
- Business qualifies for some bank financing
- Seller wants more cash upfront
- Bank requires seller financing to show confidence
```

**Structure #3: Earnout Component**
```
Purchase Price: $300,000
Down: $60,000 (20%)
Seller Note: $180,000 (60%)
Earnout: $60,000 (20% - based on performance)

Earnout structure:
- Year 1: $20K if revenue > $X
- Year 2: $20K if revenue > $Y
- Year 3: $20K if revenue > $Z

When this works:
- You're unsure about projections
- Seller is confident in growth
- Reduces your risk
- Aligns seller's interest in successful transition
```

### Seller Financing Negotiation Tips

**In Your Initial Conversations:**
1. Don't mention seller financing immediately
2. Build rapport and show you're qualified
3. After LOI, propose creative structure
4. Frame as "tax advantaged" for seller
5. Offer slightly higher interest than bank (5-7%)

**What to Negotiate:**
- **Interest rate:** 5-7% is fair (between savings and market returns)
- **Term length:** Longer is better for you (7-10 years vs. 3-5)
- **Collateral:** Note secured by business assets
- **Personal guarantee:** Try to avoid, but may be required
- **Acceleration clauses:** Understand what triggers full payment
- **Prepayment penalty:** Negotiate none (you want flexibility)

**Seller Concerns to Address:**
- "What if you stop paying?"
  - Show your financial stability (employment)
  - Offer business assets as collateral
  - Provide personal guarantee if needed
  - Show your experience and commitment

- "I need the cash now"
  - Explain tax benefits of installment sale
  - Show comparable interest to safe investments
  - Offer higher down payment if possible
  - Consider hybrid with some bank financing

### What If Seller Won't Finance?

**Alternative Creative Structures:**

1. **Investment Partner**
   - Partner puts up capital for equity stake
   - You operate (semi-absentee)
   - They get preferred return first
   - Watch: Alignment on strategy, exit

2. **SBA 7(a) Loan**
   - Can use for acquisition
   - Requires 10% down ($30K on $300K business)
   - Personal guarantee required
   - Takes 60-90 days approval
   - Seller may need to finance 10-20% for bank comfort

3. **ROBS (Rollover Business Startup)**
   - Use 401(k) funds penalty-free
   - Complex structure, need specialist
   - No debt, but depletes retirement
   - Only if you have substantial 401(k)

4. **Pass and Keep Looking**
   - Plenty of sellers will finance
   - Don't force a deal that doesn't work
   - Right deal is worth waiting for

## Due Diligence: Semi-Absentee Specific

### Critical Questions You Must Answer

**Management Verification (MOST IMPORTANT):**
1. Who runs day-to-day operations currently?
2. How long have they been in this role?
3. What's their compensation? Will they stay post-sale?
4. Do they have equity/profit sharing currently?
5. What happens if they leave? (succession plan)
6. Get to know them - are they competent and trustworthy?
7. Check: Do employees respect them?

**Owner Involvement Verification:**
1. Exactly what does owner do weekly? (Get specific list)
2. How many hours/week does owner actually work?
3. Could owner take a 3-month vacation today? (If no = not semi-absentee)
4. What relationships are owner-dependent? (customers, suppliers)
5. Are systems documented or in owner's head?

**Systems & Processes:**
1. Are procedures documented (SOPs)?
2. Can business run without owner for a week? Month?
3. Who handles: sales, operations, customer service, finance?
4. What software/systems are used?
5. Are financials updated monthly by a bookkeeper?

**Revenue Sustainability:**
1. What's the customer churn rate? (critical for recurring revenue)
2. How are new customers acquired?
3. Customer acquisition cost vs lifetime value?
4. Are there any at-risk customers or contracts?
5. What percentage of revenue is truly recurring?

### The Manager Meeting

**You MUST spend significant time with the manager:**

Schedule multiple meetings:
1. **First meeting:** With owner present
   - Understand their role
   - Gauge their interest in staying
   - Professional background

2. **Second meeting:** Without owner
   - Would they stay post-acquisition?
   - What would they change if they could?
   - What are the real problems?
   - What's their compensation expectation?
   - Career goals?

3. **Third meeting:** Shadow them for a day
   - See them actually work
   - Meet the team with them
   - Observe customer interactions
   - Verify competence

**Red Flags:**
- Manager wants to leave soon
- Manager doesn't know financials
- Team doesn't respect manager
- Manager is owner's family member who isn't qualified
- Manager is burned out
- Manager wants significant raise to stay

**Green Flags:**
- Been there 3+ years
- Team loves them
- Could run it without owner today
- Excited about potential new ownership
- Reasonable compensation for role
- Professional and organized

### Manager Retention Agreement

**Critical: Get this in writing BEFORE closing**

Key terms:
- 2-year commitment minimum
- Defined compensation (base + bonus)
- Clear role and responsibilities
- Severance terms if you terminate
- Notice period if they quit (90 days)
- Non-compete clause (1-2 years)

Consider:
- Small equity stake (5-10%) to align incentives
- Performance bonuses tied to growth
- Annual review and raise structure

## Protecting Yourself: Semi-Absentee Specific

**Risk #1: Manager Leaves Post-Closing**
**Mitigation:**
- Retention agreement with 2-year commitment
- Stay bonus paid quarterly
- Get to know them extensively pre-close
- Have backup plan (who would step up?)

- Non-compete agreement (1-2 years, reasonable geography)
- Non-solicitation of customers and employees

**Mitigation:**
- Weekly manager check-ins (video call)
- Monthly financial reviews (real-time access)
- Quarterly customer surveys
- Annual employee surveys
- Mystery shop your own business
- Build relationships with key employees (not just manager)
- Trust but verify

**Risk #4: Business Declines and You Don't Notice Until Too Late**
**Mitigation:**
- Set up automated dashboards (revenue, customers, churn)
- Weekly KPI reports from manager
- Monthly P&L review with accountant
- Quarterly deep dives
- Annual strategic planning
- Customer retention alerts

**Risk #5: Seller Didn't Disclose Problems**
**Mitigation:**
- Longer escrow holdback (18-24 months vs. 12)
- 10-15% holdback amount minimum
- Strong indemnification provisions
- Extended seller transition (6 months minimum)
- Verify everything in due diligence

7. **Legal/Compliance Issues**

   - Environmental issues
   - Employment problems

8. **You Don't Understand It**
   - Too technical for your background
   - Can't explain how they make money
   - Industry you can't grasp
   - Trust your gut
