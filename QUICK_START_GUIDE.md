## YOUR TARGET BUSINESS PROFILE

Fill this out and reference constantly:

**Financial Criteria:**
- Purchase Price: $200K - $400K (max)
- Annual Revenue: $400K - $1M
- SDE (Owner Benefit): $80K - $150K
- Profit Margin: 25%+ minimum
- Growth: Stable or growing (not declining)

**Business Model:**
- Recurring revenue: YES (critical for you)
- Customer retention: 85%+ annual
- Customer concentration: No customer >15% revenue
- Revenue model: Subscriptions, contracts, memberships preferred

**Operations:**
- Manager in place: REQUIRED (non-negotiable)
- Manager tenure: 2+ years in role
- Manager willing to stay: REQUIRED
- Owner time: <15 hours/week currently
- Documented processes: Preferred

**Industry:**
- Primary targets:
  1. SaaS/subscription software
  2. Subscription box/e-commerce
  3. Membership services (gyms, coworking, etc.)
  4. B2B service contracts (cleaning, landscaping, etc.)
  5. Digital marketing agencies (retainer clients)

- Maybe:
  - Franchises (service-based)
  - Property management

- Avoid:
  - Restaurants (too hands-on)
  - Retail (declining, hands-on)
  - Manufacturing (complex)
  - Healthcare (regulatory)

**Location:**
- San Fernando Valley (within 30 miles preferred)
- OR fully online/remote
- Willing to consider other LA areas for right deal

**Deal Structure:**
- Seller financing: HIGHLY preferred (75%+)
- Bank loan: Acceptable if needed (SBA or conventional)
- All cash: Only if screaming deal
- Earnout: Open to it

**Red Lines (Walk Away If...):**
- No manager or manager leaving
- Revenue declining 2 of last 3 years
- Owner embedded in all operations
- Customer concentration >30% top 3 customers
- Can't verify financials
- Legal/compliance issues
- Seller won't provide transition support

## ATTORNEY INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

When meeting with M&A attorneys:

1. How many small business acquisitions have you handled in the last 2 years?
2. What's the typical size of deals you work on?
3. Have you done deals with significant seller financing?
4. What's your fee structure? (Flat fee vs hourly)
5. For a $300K acquisition, what would your fees be approximately?
6. Will you review my LOI before I submit it?
7. What are the biggest mistakes you see buyers make?
8. Can you help with purchase agreement negotiation or just review?
9. Do you have experience with SaaS/online business acquisitions? (if relevant)
10. References from other acquisition clients?

**What you want to hear:**
- 10+ acquisitions in last 2 years
- $100K-$5M deal size range
- Flat fee or capped fee ($5K-$10K range)
- Will actively help negotiate, not just review
- Proactive about protecting you

**Red flags:**
- Only done big deals ($10M+) - you'll be too small
- Never done seller financing deals
- Hourly with no cap (could get expensive)
- Can't provide references

## CPA/ACCOUNTANT INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

1. How many business acquisition clients have you worked with?
2. Can you help analyze the seller's financials for red flags?
3. Do you understand quality of earnings analysis?
4. Can you help structure the deal for best tax outcome?
5. Asset sale vs stock sale - which is better for me and why?
6. What's your fee for acquisition support?
7. After acquisition, can you handle monthly bookkeeping/financials?
8. Do you work with business owners who have a separate W2 job?
9. What are the biggest financial red flags I should watch for?
10. References?

**What you want to hear:**
- Experience with acquisitions (even if just a few)
- Understands add-backs and SDE calculations
- Can explain asset vs stock clearly
- Reasonable fees ($2K-$5K for transaction)
- Available for ongoing support post-acquisition

## COMMON EARLY MISTAKES TO AVOID

### Mistake #1: Moving Too Fast
**Wrong:** "I found a business! Let's buy it!" (Week 2)
**Right:** Review 100+ businesses before getting serious on one

### Mistake #2: Analysis Paralysis
**Wrong:** Month 6, reviewed 300 businesses, submitted zero LOIs
**Right:** Submit LOI on marginal deal just for practice (month 2-3)

### Mistake #3: Not Building Relationships
**Wrong:** Just browsing listings, never talking to anyone
**Right:** Weekly conversations with brokers, sellers, other buyers

### Mistake #4: Skipping Due Diligence
**Wrong:** "Financials look good, let's close!"
**Right:** Verify everything, meet manager 3+ times, review 3 years records

### Mistake #5: Falling in Love With a Deal
**Wrong:** "I NEED this business, I'll pay asking price"
**Right:** Walk away from 90% of deals. Right deal will feel right AND make sense

### Mistake #6: Ignoring Your Criteria
**Wrong:** "I said semi-absentee, but I'll run this restaurant myself..."
**Right:** Stick to your criteria ruthlessly

### Mistake #7: No Seller Financing
**Wrong:** Accepting "all cash only" from seller
**Right:** Walk away or negotiate creative structure. Seller should have skin in game.

## WHEN TO SUBMIT YOUR FIRST LOI

**Submit LOI when ALL of these are true:**
- ✅ Financials verify (reviewed 3 years tax returns + P&L)
- ✅ Met with seller 2+ times (phone/video OK for online business)
- ✅ Business fits your criteria (don't compromise on musts)
- ✅ Manager exists and seems solid (for semi-absentee)
- ✅ Industry makes sense to you
- ✅ Numbers work (cash flow supports debt + pays you)
- ✅ Your gut says "yes" (trust instinct)
- ✅ No major red flags
- ✅ Seller seems reasonable and honest
- ✅ You'd be excited to own this

**Don't submit LOI if ANY of these:**
- ❌ Haven't seen tax returns
- ❌ Haven't talked to seller
- ❌ Major red flags present
- ❌ Numbers don't work
- ❌ You're settling because you're impatient
- ❌ Manager situation is unclear
- ❌ Gut says "something's off"
